.about

Who I am

I'm a master's student in the Department of Computer Science and Engineering at Sungkyunkwan University, working in SecAI Lab under Prof. Hyungjoon (Kevin) Koo. I joined the lab in Fall 2024.

Most of what I do involves reading code that was never meant to be read: stripped binaries, aggressively optimized builds, deliberately obfuscated targets. I'm interested in the seam where automated analysis gives up and someone still has to sit down with a disassembler — and in how much of that gap we can actually close.

The object of study

0x00007FFFFFFFFFFF
[stack] rw-↓ grows toward lower addresses
[mmap] ld.so, libc rwx↓ top-down allocation
[heap] rw-↑ grows via brk / sbrk
.bss rw-zero-filled at load
.data rw-initialized, writable
.rodata r--strings — often the only foothold
.text r-xno names attached — this is the work
0x0000555555554000
unmappednull page — deref faults
0x0000000000000000

x86-64 Linux, 4-level paging (48-bit virtual addresses). High addresses at top: the stack
descends into the gap while the heap climbs into it. Load base shown is the PIE base with
ASLR disabled; in normal operation it is randomized on every exec.

Notice what is missing. .symtab and .strtab are not SHF_ALLOC — they never enter the process image at all, only the file on disk. Strip the binary and they are gone from that too.

So everything downstream starts from what survives: executable bytes with no names attached to them, and whatever .rodata happens to leak. Most of my work is about how much of the missing half can be reconstructed, and how confident anyone is entitled to be about the reconstruction.
